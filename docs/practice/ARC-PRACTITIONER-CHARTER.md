# Arc Practitioner Charter

## Purpose

This charter defines the emerging Arc Practitioner role without presenting a completed certification program or granting domain, legal, security, or operational authority.

An Arc Practitioner works against a body of work, not merely against a software installation.

> **An Arc Practitioner establishes and stewards a durable workplace around an important body of work, making it possible for changing combinations of people and intelligence to contribute while the organization's understanding, authority, evidence, and ability to continue remain intact.**

## The practitioner's objective

The practitioner should leave the body of work:

- more understandable;
- more accurately represented;
- more governable;
- less dependent on private memory;
- easier for a new qualified participant to continue;
- clearer about what is known, inferred, disputed, stale, missing, or unknown;
- better able to distinguish working output from accepted state;
- better able to direct human and machine attention toward material uncertainty.

The practitioner is not judged by how much process or documentation they create. They are judged by the condition of the workplace and the quality of transfer.

## Core responsibilities

### Bound the work

- Identify a coherent body of work whose continuity matters.
- State its purpose, scope, ownership, dependencies, and exclusions.
- Avoid boundaries so broad that they become ungovernable or so narrow that they collapse into one temporary task.
- Record the relationship to adjacent bodies of work without automatically combining them.

### Recover and maintain the Situation

- Collect relevant artifacts, records, system observations, interviews, and active work.
- Separate direct evidence, attributed testimony, technical inference, current decisions, and unknowns.
- Identify stale or contradictory material.
- Keep the current Situation concise enough to orient a newcomer while preserving routes to underlying evidence.
- Avoid converting a plausible reconstruction into false historical certainty.

### Establish truth and acceptance

- Help the organization define what counts as Canon and what remains working state.
- Make acceptance criteria and evidence expectations explicit.
- Preserve disagreements and unresolved questions where certainty is not warranted.
- Ensure that persistence alone does not make a claim authoritative.
- Make supersession, expiration, correction, and retraction possible.

### Establish roles and authority

- Map who may commission, perform, verify, attest, approve, and Commit different forms of Work.
- Preserve separation among judgment, authorization, execution, verification, and acceptance.
- Ensure that agents and humans receive only the context and authority appropriate to their role.
- Escalate material ambiguity rather than encouraging participants to guess through it.

### Conduct meaningful Work

- Translate important needs into bounded Commissions and observable Outcome Contracts.
- Select appropriate human, agent, and deterministic performers.
- Keep Work useful and proportionate rather than ceremonial.
- Preserve artifacts, events, decisions, limitations, and resource use sufficient for another participant to understand what occurred.
- Ensure failed or partial Work leaves useful evidence rather than disappearing.

### Accrete useful knowledge

- Propose accepted decisions, procedures, assertions, artifacts, negative results, benchmarks, and open questions for future reuse.
- Preserve provenance, scope, freshness, assumptions, and limitations.
- Avoid replaying raw transcripts as the default form of organizational memory.
- Prevent a finding from one body of work from silently becoming universal organizational truth.

### Test continuity

- Run a Continuity Handoff Test with a participant who lacks the practitioner's private context.
- Observe where the participant becomes confused, overconfident, blocked, or dependent on undocumented explanation.
- Treat handoff failures as defects in the workplace, not merely shortcomings in the newcomer.
- Improve the environment and repeat the test where material failures remain.

### Transfer stewardship

- Prepare an internal Arc Steward or practice group to maintain the workplace.
- Externalize procedures the steward will need.
- Make pending decisions, risks, evidence gaps, and maintenance obligations visible.
- Avoid making the practitioner permanently indispensable.
- Leave a clear path for future correction and evolution.

## Role separation

An Arc Practitioner may also hold another role, but the roles remain logically distinct.

| Role | Primary responsibility |
|---|---|
| **Arc Practitioner** | Establishes and improves the durable work environment |
| **Arc Steward** | Maintains an established environment over time |
| **Domain Expert** | Supplies subject-matter knowledge |
| **Domain Attestor** | Renders a qualified judgment about an exact subject |
| **Authorized Principal** | Exercises organizational authority |
| **Verifier** | Reproduces or checks an observable claim |
| **Platform Operator** | Operates infrastructure and services |
| **Performer** | Conducts a bounded unit of Work |

A practitioner working in a domain does not automatically become qualified to attest to that domain. A practitioner who structures an approval path does not automatically possess approval authority.

## Practice principles

### 1. Continuity over dependency

The workplace should become less dependent on any individual, vendor, model, or tool, including the practitioner.

### 2. Truthfulness over completeness theater

State what is known and how it is known. Preserve unknowns. Do not imply complete lineage where only partial evidence exists.

### 3. Current state over archival volume

A large archive is not a current Situation. Preserve evidence, but orient participants toward what matters now.

### 4. Evidence over narrative authority

A polished summary does not outrank its sources. Claims should remain connected to evidence, attribution, and limitations.

### 5. Proportionate structure

Use the least process sufficient to preserve continuity, authority, and truth. Arc Practice should reduce reconstruction work, not add ceremony to trivial tasks.

### 6. Explicit authority

Do not confuse participation, expertise, approval, attestation, verification, and Commit.

### 7. Bounded capture

Do not collect every conversation or artifact merely because collection is possible. Consider privacy, consent, confidentiality, retention, and future relevance.

### 8. Scoped reuse

Knowledge should travel only where its provenance, policy, domain, and assumptions make reuse appropriate.

### 9. Human and agent legibility

The workplace should be understandable to qualified humans and machine performers without requiring hidden context.

### 10. Correctability

Every accepted state must be capable of correction, supersession, retraction, or expiration through an explicit process.

### 11. Independent value

Arc Practice must leave the body of work materially better whether or not the organization subsequently purchases, continues, or expands any amotivv product or service. Successful practice is measured by durable improvement to the work, not vendor conversion.

A practitioner may identify a managed Attexa Arc or Agent Anything deployment as an appropriate continuation path, but conversion is a commercial outcome, not the success condition of the engagement. A non-converting engagement can still be successful Arc Practice when it leaves the organization with a more legible, governable, continuable body of work.

## Ethical commitments

An Arc Practitioner should:

- protect confidential, personal, customer, partner, and employee information;
- obtain appropriate consent and authority before bringing material into the workplace;
- avoid turning surveillance into “continuity”;
- disclose conflicts of interest;
- distinguish their own interpretation from organizationally accepted state;
- respect domain and professional boundaries;
- avoid overstating security, verification, or completeness;
- preserve meaningful dissent and uncertainty;
- make handoff assessment fair and role-appropriate;
- leave organizations capable of continuing without proprietary practitioner knowledge;
- preserve the independent value of the engagement rather than treating product conversion as proof of practice success.

## Evidence of competent practice

A future practitioner practicum should contain at least:

```text
Body-of-Work Charter
Initial Situation and uncertainty map
Canon and working-state definitions
Role and authority model
One or more real Commissions
Human, agent, or deterministic Attempts
Outcomes and evidence
Commit decisions
Accepted and rejected Candidate knowledge
Final Situation
Continuity Handoff Test
Known limitations
Practitioner reflection
Assessment record
```

Possessing templates is not evidence of competent practice. The resulting workplace must enable a qualified newcomer to understand and continue useful Work.

## Success condition

> **An Arc Practitioner succeeds when the body of work becomes less dependent on the practitioner's private knowledge.**

A practitioner who remains the only person capable of explaining or operating the environment has created a new dependency rather than durable continuity.

Independent value is part of that success condition. The organization should retain meaningful improvement in the body of work even when it does not adopt a particular amotivv product or continue a commercial relationship with the practitioner.

## Certification boundary

**No Attexa Certified Arc Practitioner credential is currently offered.**

If a credential is later created, it should:

- require an end-to-end assessed practicum;
- publish a competency rubric;
- separate Arc competence from domain competence;
- require evidence of continuity handoff;
- include conflict-of-interest and ethical requirements;
- be renewable and revocable;
- avoid implying production, security, legal, or domain assurance outside the assessed scope.

Arc Practice remains open for use without certification. A future certification would establish assessed competence, not permission to use the method.
